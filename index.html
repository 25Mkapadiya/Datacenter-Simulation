    How this works

    This tool estimates whether a proposed datacenter site in Texas is buildable, using real public infrastructure data. Click anywhere on the map to evaluate a site.

    Every site is checked through five gates, in order:

    1. Power — is there transmission/generation infrastructure nearby? (real data, but an estimate — see below)
    2. Fiber — network connectivity (not wired to real data yet)
    3. Regulation — local pauses, moratoria, and state law (real, hand-researched)
    4. Water — estimated demand from published coefficients
    5. Land — reference cost, not site-specific

    A site that fails an early gate is blocked, no matter how well it would score on a later one — a great location for water access doesn't help if there's no power nearby.

    The single most important number for real siting — how much spare capacity a substation actually has — is legally non-public information (it's CEII-restricted). So the "Power" gate estimates it instead, from nearby generation capacity in public EIA data. Every score this tool gives you is directional, not authoritative — click the ⓘ chips throughout for plain-language definitions as you go, and see the "Explore the data" panel below the map to look at the underlying dataset yourself.
